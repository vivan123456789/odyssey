## The Default Problem

When you say "we're raising money for charity" at a show, the next question is usually "which one?" And more often than not, the answer is one of the same five or ten household names that get the bulk of donated dollars every year.

That's not a knock on those organizations. Many of them do important work. But there's a long tail of smaller, scrappier, higher-leverage initiatives that don't get airtime — because they don't have marketing budgets, because they're operating in niches most people don't know exist, because they haven't cracked the visibility problem.

[summer larp] thinks live music is an interesting way to address that.

## The Attention Argument

A show is a moment when a room of people is paying attention. That's rare and valuable. Most fundraising asks people to give money to something they already know about. We want to use that moment differently — to introduce an organization or cause that the audience genuinely might not have encountered before.

If 100 people come to a show and 20 of them walk out knowing about an organization they'd never heard of, that's a different kind of impact than collecting $500 for a cause everyone already donates to. Both matter. We're choosing to optimize for the second.

## What We're Looking For

When we select a charity partner for a show or series of shows, we're asking:

- Is this organization doing something that would otherwise go underfunded?
- Does the cause have a clear, traceable connection between donations and outcomes?
- Is there a story here that a live music audience would connect with if they heard it?
- Is this an organization that actually needs the spotlight, not just the dollars?

We're not setting a minimum size requirement. We're not filtering for name recognition. We're looking for signal — high-impact work in a context that most fundraising events overlook.

## How It Works at Shows

At each show, we'll share a brief intro to the charity partner before or between sets. QR codes for donations will be posted. After the event, we'll publish a breakdown of what was raised and where it went.

We want the process to be transparent so that audiences trust it. The goal is for people to leave knowing they contributed to something specific, not just "charity."
